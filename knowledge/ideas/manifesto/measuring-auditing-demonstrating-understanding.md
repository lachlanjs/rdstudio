We are in a position where an LLM can understand a complex project better than we can. We should leverage this in order to enhance our own understanding.

### How do you know you have attempted to understand

As a matter of honesty, we can't expect to understand something without genuinely trying. Fortunately, we can force ourselves to be honest about this through tracking our own attempts at building our understanding through the methods listed above. With minimal effort we can produce tangible rewards which we can reflect on and communicate to others.

### How do you know that you do understand

There is an ingredient of honesty in this for which there is no real measure outside of one's own introspection. Understanding is somewhat of a slippery thing to define and conceptualise in and of itself, but we can measure things akin to understanding through deterministic proxies such as recitation.

Ultimately, the quality of one's understanding will be demonstrated through its use in application to new problems.

### How do you audit your understanding

Understanding is a dynamic attribute. Understanding may change both in status and in nature. One might be tempted to believe that certifying understanding once upon a time is sufficient, when it often isn't. 

The side benefit of auditing your understanding is receiving a clear signal of the limitations of your own finite mental bandwidth. Should you notice that your understanding is drifting, decaying, or losing currency, you may take this warning as inspiration to warrant a reduction in your workload.

### How do you demonstrate your understanding

We exist within the structures of corporations, which organise themselves through traditional value structures built on tangible deliverables and artifacts. As workplaces adjust their value structures from material output (codebases, reports, etc) towards weighting understanding higher, we must bridge this transition with tangible and demonstrable attestations of that understanding.

Education systems have long-standing traditions of examination, wherein subject matter experts construct (in secret to pupils) tests of knowledge. As we begin to look up to AI systems as having greater understanding than our own, even within facets of our own projects, we may turn to AI systems to provide these forms of examination.

We can also tour others, such as colleagues, managers, and stakeholders, through our system of understanding. This is yet another intervention through which we can test understanding, though the pressure is greater.

### Proposed Intervention: Factual Recollection

A great body of literature exists in studying efficient systems for rote learning and recall (think Anki and rates of memory decay over time, spaced repetition, etc). Measuring the user's ability to factually recollect pieces of information can easily be done and provides a clear quantitative ranking of the individual's knowledge. 

### Proposed Intervention: AI-driven Examination

The world is exploring various ways in which AI can augment education. Schools and universities are deploying chatbots (an uninspired approach). Some institutions are leaving an AGENTS.md in the root to define a tutor role in the student's harness. Some individuals are building specialised systems (somewhat like I am) in order to help them learn. These often involve some form of examination, with tiers of difficulty managed by the AI to help gauge understanding, detect weaknesses, and inform lesson plans. Something similar could be worked into this system. 

### Proposed Intervention: Feedback and Reward

We are reinforcement learning machines driven greatly by reward. We must imbue a learning system with inherent motivational rewards that correspond to the event of learning taking place.

As with building understanding, measurement of understanding can and should be done multidimensionally.
